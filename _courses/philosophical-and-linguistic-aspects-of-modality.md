## Course Overview

This seminar follows a conceptual path from the foundational philosophical debates over modal notions to the development of technical tools for the analysis of modality in linguistics and formal semantics. It reflects not only the historical evolution of philosophical problems surrounding modality, but also the contemporary approach to modal expressions in natural languages, offering an empirically informed discussion of modality across the issues relevant to metaphysics and philosophy of language from both a foundational and a descriptive perspective.

## Prerequisites

Familiarity with first-order logic and basic propositional modal logic is recommended. Familiarity with lambda calculus and truth-conditional semantics is helpful but will be reviewed.

## Core Topics

### Part 1: Metaphysical modality

- Strict modality, the semantic conception, and the Barcan Marcus–Quine debate
- The new theory of reference and Kripke's conception of metaphysical necessity
- *A posteriori* necessity and Aristotelian essentialism
- Metaphysical and epistemological debates about modal facts
- Descriptivism, anti-factualism, and Thomasson's modal normativism

### Part 2: Modality in natural languages

- Foundations of contemporary formal and intensional semantics
- The Kratzerian framework for modality
- Epistemic, deontic, and ability modals
- Weak and strong necessity modals
- Event relativity in the interpretation of natural language modals
- Causal structure, ability modals, and actuality entailments

### Part 3: Applications of modal semantics

- Modal operators and semantic weakening/strengthening mechanisms
- Syntactic structure and the interpretation of modal expressions
- Modality in the nominal domain
