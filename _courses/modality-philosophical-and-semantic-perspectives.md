## Course Overview

This course articulates a conceptual path from the foundational philosophical debates over modal notions, to the development of technical tools for the semantic analysis of modals and conditionals. The course not only reflects the historical evolution of philosophical issues surrounding modality, but their projection into contemporary, formal accounts as well, in an attempt to make explicit the conceptual tension that exists between factual and non-factual views of both modality and conditionals, which also underlies the mathematical tools used to provide semantic characterizations of those notions.

## Core Topics

### Part 1: Philosophical debates about modality

- Strict modality and the semantic conception
- Quine's critique of modal logic
- Kripke's metaphysical turn
- Thomasson's modal normativism

### Part 2: Modals and conditionals in formal semantics

- Modality as a notional category
- The Kratzerian framework for modality
- Epistemic and deontic
- Event relativity in the interpretation of natural language modals

### Part 3: Modals and conditionals in dynamic semantics

- Dynamic semantics, static semantics, and dynamic pragmatics
- Epistemic modals in update semantics
- Deontic modals in update semantics
- Event anaphora in the interpretation of natural language modals
